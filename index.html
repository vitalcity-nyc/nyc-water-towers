New York City infrastructure

The wooden water towers above us

More than 5,600 registered rooftop water tanks dot the five boroughs. City water pressure climbs only about six stories on its own — above that, gravity from these cedar and redwood barrels does the rest. Three family-run firms have built nearly all of them, using cooperage techniques first deployed in the late 1800s.

Wood holds because it insulates against freezing in winter, stays cooler in summer, and swells when wet — sealing a tank watertight without nails or synthetic sealants. A typical barrel holds 5,000 to 10,000 gallons, is assembled on a rooftop in a single day from precut staves, and lasts roughly 25 to 35 years before it needs replacing.
