NYC water towers

New York City has more than 5,600 registered rooftop water tanks -- the wooden, barrel-shaped icons visible from any vantage point. They exist because city water pressure only reaches about six stories. Above that, gravity from these tanks does the work.

The builders

A craft passed down for generations

Nearly all of NYC's wooden water towers are built and maintained by just three companies, two of them family-run since the 19th century. Rosenwach Tank Co. (founded 1866) and Isseks Brothers (founded 1880s) still hand-build tanks from cedar and redwood -- the same way it was done when the first skyscrapers went up. American Pipe & Tank rounds out the trio.

Why they are still made of wood

Cedar and redwood, no sealants needed

Wood is a natural insulator -- it keeps water from freezing in winter without heaters. When the staves get wet, they swell, making the tank watertight without any toxic sealants. A well-maintained wooden tank lasts 25-35 years. Steel tanks exist but are rarer, more expensive, and require liners.

How they work

Gravity does the heavy lifting

Pumps in the basement push water up to the rooftop tank (typically holding 5,000 to 10,000 gallons). From there, gravity feeds water back down to every floor. A float valve triggers the pump when the level drops. The system also provides the pressure needed for fire sprinklers.
